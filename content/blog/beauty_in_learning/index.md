For me, learning new stuff isn't always as fun as I'd like it to be! Sometimes it's the deadlines, other times it's just the nature of the subject/topic I'm learning not being interesting enough, and many times, it's also a lack of truly deep understanding of what I'm learning working on. All of them are completely fine, especially the last one as I don't always have the bandwidth to do deep dives and a working understanding is only what I require to accomplish the final goal (eg. code change, bug fix, a working implementation of something that's deeper than what's required to get value out of it). But sometimes, I come across stuff that feels just beautiful to learn! 

This is my running list of those things -- stuff that I've loved learning to an extent that's beyond what I can describe accurately. Pure joy if I may!

*Note: This list is not at all meant to "fully" describe or restrict my interests, or define my preferences, or be treated as my resume. There's many things outside of this list that I have a very good understanding of, and care to work on.*

1. Linear Algebra via the [book](https://3d.bk.tudelft.nl/courses/geo5017/books/Linear_algebra.pdf) Schaum's linear algebra. I finished this complete book in a week time in the year 2020 while preparing for an entrance test. 

Was the first time I learnt about Vector spaces (but wasn't the first time I learnt about matrices obviously -- that a standard 8/9 thing in ISC board in India). But this was the first time I understood the significance of matrices in great detail beyond abstract concepts. 

2. Reinforcement learning [videos](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLqYmG7hTraZDM-OYHWgPebj2MfCFzFObQ) by David Silver. How cringe/cliche/copied would it sound if I say the best way to think about the problem RL theoretically is to think of humans as agents interacting with an env called life...swear this isn't copied, and agreed, it's a bit unnecessary lol. These lectures had kept me hooked, and I really love thinking about RL as a field. <3
